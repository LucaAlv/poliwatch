# Data licence and attribution

This file states the terms for the data of Bundestag-Puls (working title): the SQLite file, the CSV tables, the recipe results and `DATA.md` of every data export, and the figures the site shows. The project's code is under the MIT licence (`LICENSE` in the repository); that licence does not cover the data.

Every data export carries this file as `LICENSE-DATA.md` next to its `DATA.md`, which also quotes it from „Short string" on. The decision behind it is ADR 0003 in the project repository (`docs/adr/0003-licences-and-impressum.md`), which also lists the questions still open for a lawyer.

Aufbereitet und verändert; Details in LICENSE-DATA.md.

## Terms per layer

The file combines sources with different terms. Each column's licence is the layer `DATA.md` gives it (column „Lizenzschicht", authored in `scripts/data_dictionary.json`). The `source` tag in `data/exports/datenstand.json` (`tables[].columns[].source`) is the coarser origin class in the table below; the export fails when a column's layer does not allow its tag (`LICENSE_LAYER_SOURCES`). That check catches cross-origin contradictions only: the CC BY metadata layers also allow `dip` and `bundestag.de` tags, so whether a column is metadata or content rests on the authored layer. Our own ids and timestamps on source tables are project metadata under CC BY 4.0 even where the tag names the source.

The DIP terms say in no. 5 that any use of DIP data is forbidden that happens in a distorting context or could disparage members of the Bundestag, the Bundesrat, the government or others. This is the source's notice, passed on as it stands: a condition of the source, not a term this file adds, and the CC BY 4.0 grant for our own work carries no such condition.

Two columns of the `derived` row quote the wording of a source: `documents.forderung` (the Antrag passage a vote quotes; `vote_passages` holds only the ids and order of the documents it points to) and `votes.inversion_excerpt` (the clause, title or description that shows a recommendation to reject). Our selection and extraction of these passages is our own work under CC BY 4.0. The quoted wording itself stays under the DIP layer, with the attribution in the table above and the change notice below (for an excerpt of the Bundestag's vote pages, under the `bundestag.de` row).

## What we changed (DIP no. 4b)

- The protocol XML and DIP API records are extracted and split into tables. `speeches.text` and `contributions.text` are the spoken text with whitespace normalised: non-breaking spaces become plain spaces, and runs of whitespace inside a paragraph collapse to one space. The paragraphs (without the speaker line) are separated by one line feed (`\n`), and the text holds no other. They are the paragraphs the protocol prints: where it interrupts one for applause or a Zwischenruf and goes on without an indent (`<p klasse="O">` after a `<kommentar>`), the paragraph continues across the part left out. A block quote the speaker reads out (`<p klasse="Z">`; in WP 18 about half of these stand inside `<zitat>`) is part of the text, as paragraphs of its own. Wording and punctuation are not edited. The duplicate `paragraphs_json` column is dropped at export.
- `speeches.text` and `contributions.text` hold only the speaker's own words. What the protocol prints inside a Rede as its own element and that is not the speaker's is left out: applause, Zwischenrufe and other reactions (`<kommentar>`), and the remarks of the Sitzungsleitung. A turn of another speaker inside the Rede (a Zwischenfrage, a Kurzintervention, an answer) is not part of the Rede's text either; where the parser recognises it, it is stored as that speaker's Beitrag in `contributions` (inside a withheld Rede, in `withheld_turns`). So a Rede's text is shorter than the passage in the protocol. One exception: in 18 Reden and one Beitrag the protocol prints a reaction inside a paragraph of the speaker, not as its own element, and it stays in the text. In the data of 2026-11-22 (806 sittings) these are, by protocol number and then `speeches.rede_id`: with „(Beifall …)“ the Reden 18/92 ID1809200100, 18/122 ID1812201600, 18/124 ID1812400200, 18/127 ID1812705400, 18/174 ID1817401100, 19/89 ID198905400, 19/110 ID1911000200, 19/130 ID1913005300, 19/137 ID1913713700, 19/146 ID1914613800, 19/155 ID1915509200, 20/45 ID204504800 and 20/163 ID2016309300, and the Beitrag 21/49 ID214902800 (`contributions.rede_id`); with a „(Zuruf …)“ or a named Zwischenruf „(Name [Fraktion]: …)“ the Reden 18/127 ID1812705400 (which also keeps a Beifall), 18/231 ID1823110000, 18/233 ID1823307900, 19/76 ID197608000 and 19/112 ID1911205300; and with the remark „(Abg. … meldet sich zu einer Zwischenfrage)“ the Rede 19/182 ID1918209300. The protocol itself, cited as „BT-PlPr.“ with its number (`protocols.document_number`) and page (`speeches.page`), has the full passage.
- The parser classifies each turn of the protocol. `speeches` holds Reden only. Kurzinterventionen, Erwiderungen, mündliche Erklärungen, and the Fragen and Antworten of a Befragung or Fragestunde are stored as Beiträge in `contributions` (`kind`, `parent_rede_id`, `sprechrolle`). The protocol does not label these kinds as data: the classification is ours, made from the wording and structure of the XML and, for single turns, from a reviewed list that also used DIP's Aktivitäten; it is not free of errors. A Beitrag the parser missed is still in `speeches`.
- Fraktion spellings are normalised to one form per Fraktion (`parties.name`, `speeches.fraktion`).
  - `speeches.fraktion` is the Fraktion the protocol names for that Rede, i.e. the affiliation at the time of the speech.
  - `mps.party_id` is the affiliation as of the last build.
  - `speeches.fraktion` is `NULL` when the XML names no Fraktion (a minister speaking in role).
- Persons from the DIP roster, the protocol XML and the roll-call lists are consolidated into one person id by the person registry (`persons`, `person_aliases`, `person_records`, `person_bindings`; `mp_canonical` maps every source record to its person). Its purpose is that the exported „Rezepte" counts match the site's profile pages.
- A roll-call vote is stored once per source vote. `votes.protocol_id` preserves its sitting even when no agenda item could be attributed; `agenda_item_votes` remains the optional topic link. Topic-based facts retain an unknown topic for these votes, while sitting-level vote counts and member histories still include them.
- `mp_canonical` and `datenstand` are created by `scripts/build_dip_pulse_site.py` during export. The tables of the website's Fakt der Woche, which the build computes from the data above, are not part of the export.
- `proceedings.abstract` is DIP's Vorgang abstract with the HTML stripped and whitespace normalised: a line break (`<br>`) or block element ends a paragraph, paragraphs are separated by a blank line (`\n\n`), other markup such as `<strong>` is removed with its text kept, entities are decoded, and runs of whitespace inside a paragraph collapse to one space. Wording is not edited. Only the first 10,000 characters are read (the longest on the reference cache has 5,111), and a numeric character reference past U+10FFFF reads as U+FFFD.
- `documents.forderung` is the operative part of an Antrag or Entschließungsantrag ("Der (Deutsche) Bundestag fordert die Bundesregierung … auf"), extracted from the Drucksache's DIP full text (`drucksache-text`) for votes whose Vorgang has no DIP abstract (`scripts/antrag_text.py`). The extractor aims to preserve wording and order while removing layout artifacts; the [local fidelity audit](https://github.com/LucaAlv/poliwatch/blob/main/docs/research/b4-antrag-extraction.md#run-3-local-reference-and-mid-period-validation-2026-10-29) recorded three historical passages that violated that aim; the extractor now refuses two of them and quotes the third without the printer's imprint, and the [Run 4 re-audit](https://github.com/LucaAlv/poliwatch/blob/main/docs/research/b4-antrag-extraction.md#run-4-source-fidelity-and-interposed-anchors-2026-10-31-extractor-7-to-9) finds no incorrect passage on that audited 91-document corpus. Fidelity beyond that corpus is not proven. The [Bundestag's usage terms](https://www.bundestag.de/services/impressum) identify Drucksachen as amtliche Werke under § 5 Abs. 2 UrhG, subject to the alteration and attribution rules in §§ 62 and 63. [DIP terms 4a–c](https://dip.bundestag.de/documents/nutzungsbedingungen_dip.pdf) allow attributed excerpts and processing of API/extracted data with changes identified. The panel identifies the excerpt, sponsors and Drucksache number, links its PDF, and names DIP as the source; the source's third-party-rights reservation in clause 6 still applies.
- `votes.inversion_excerpt` quotes the evidence for a recommendation to reject. The chair's clause in the protocol and the remark of a DIP Beschlussfassung (with its Drucksache number in front) have their whitespace normalised and are cut to 300 characters, ending in „…“; a title or description from the Bundestag's vote pages or a DIP Drucksache title is quoted whole. Wording is not edited otherwise.
- The site's machine-generated summaries are not part of the export.
