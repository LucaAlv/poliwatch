# Design: One Public Product, Optional AI Summary

Generated by /office-hours on 2026-09-14

Status: APPROVED
Mode: Builder

Implementation note (2026-09-19): the release target moved from `0.3.0` to `0.5.0.0` after `main` independently shipped `0.3.0.0` and `0.4.0.0`. The promised one-release compatibility window therefore runs through `0.5.x`, with removal deferred to `0.6.0`; historical review text below retains the version numbers used when the plan was approved.

## Problem Statement

The Baustein system was introduced to let visitors choose how much of Bundestag-Puls they see. In practice, the first-time `core-only` view hides public features that make the site useful, while the gear menu asks visitors to make product-architecture decisions before they know what the hidden sections contain. The same `Feature` model now controls build dependencies, data acquisition, page generation, navigation, CSS visibility, browser persistence, and developer tools. That multiplies implementation and test combinations without serving a likely user need.

The product should instead publish one intentional public experience. Votes, bills, profiles, MP pages, dossiers, and source links appear whenever the publication has meaningful data for them. Operators retain control over network access, cost, and enrichment. AI summaries remain separately optional because they introduce provider cost and a different trust boundary.

## What Makes This Cool

The page stops feeling like a configurable data portal and starts feeling like an edited product. A visitor can move from a plain-language account of a debate to speakers, votes, legislation, and the original Drucksache without first discovering or configuring Bausteine. The one remaining content preference is honest and legible: show or collapse clearly labelled AI summaries without changing the source-backed product underneath.

## Constraints

- Trust is the primary design constraint. AI-generated public-interest text must be visibly distinguishable from source-backed facts and remain one click from its cited passages.
- The site is statically generated. Visitor interactions must work without a server account and must degrade safely when JavaScript or `localStorage` is unavailable.
- Optional enrichment can require network access or third-party APIs. A full public presentation must not imply that every build performs every network request.
- Missing upstream data, partial enrichment, and a genuine absence of an event are different states. The interface must not collapse them into the same empty result.
- Existing build and renderer modules are valuable boundaries. Simplifying the visitor experience does not justify moving everything into the already large site-builder script.
- Existing bookmarks to `settings.html` and stored `bundestag-pulse-features` preferences must fail harmlessly during migration.
- The approved wireframe is stored alongside this document as [`fixed-public-experience-wireframe.png`](fixed-public-experience-wireframe.png). It specifies hierarchy and interaction, not final styling.

## Research Basis

- Nielsen Norman Group's progressive-disclosure guidance says frequently needed content belongs in the initial view; secondary or rarely used detail belongs behind disclosure controls: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/
- Its hidden-navigation study found lower discovery, greater perceived difficulty, and longer task times when destinations were hidden: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/
- The W3C disclosure pattern requires a real button, keyboard activation, and accurate `aria-expanded` state: https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/disclosure/
- European Commission guidance for AI Act Article 50 says people must be informed when exposed to AI-generated public-interest text without human review or editorial control. Exact legal applicability requires separate confirmation, but explicit human-readable and machine-readable labelling is the safe product default: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/guidelines-ai-transparency-obligations

## Premises

1. Public source-backed capabilities are the product, not visitor preferences. They should be rendered and discoverable by default.
2. Build-time acquisition and publication-time presentation are separate concerns. Network, cost, and dependency controls remain operator-facing.
3. AI summaries are the only visitor-controlled content layer. When present they are visible by default, clearly labelled, cited, and collapsible without hiding source-backed content.
4. Data readiness remains part of the publication contract, but appears contextually as `ready`, `partial`, `unavailable`, or a domain-specific empty state rather than as switch availability.
5. Developer output is not a public Baustein. It requires a separate, explicit developer-only path and must never leak local paths or sensitive build context into a public build.
6. Progressive disclosure belongs within sections for long evidence, raw excerpts, and method details. It should not conceal public sections or destinations.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Full-view default, retain Bausteine

Set all public features to visible by default but retain the gear, general `localStorage` overrides, client dependency resolution, and settings page. This is the smallest change, but it preserves the unwanted option model and most of its engineering cost. Rejected because it treats the symptom rather than the cause.

### Approach B: Separate operator capabilities from visitor presentation

Keep modular Python components and build-time dependency resolution for enrichments. Remove public feature switches and render source-backed experiences unconditionally. Replace the global settings system with contextual readiness messaging, add one accessible AI-summary preference, and move developer behavior behind a dedicated build-time path. This is the selected approach.

### Approach C: Remove the registry and modules

Inline every enrichment and renderer path into the site builder. This removes Baustein terminology but re-couples acquisition, persistence, and rendering inside an already large script. Rejected because it discards useful modularity without improving the visitor outcome beyond Approach B.

## Recommended Approach

Use Approach B and make the separation explicit in names and interfaces.

### Product contract

- The global header contains stable public destinations and no Baustein gear.
- Every applicable public section is rendered independently of browser attributes and stored feature overrides. Existing page-specific information hierarchy remains intact; this change fixes navigation and removes feature gating rather than forcing every page into one identical template.
- Applicability is semantic: for example, a debate page can have a vote section while an MP page cannot. Once a section is applicable, only the state rules below determine whether it shows data, an empty state, a coverage warning, or—in the special case of absent AI output—nothing.
- `settings.html` becomes a compatibility page throughout version `0.3.x`. It explains the fixed experience, states that old preferences no longer apply, and links to `sources.html#datenstand`. It contains no feature switches and is removed in `0.4.0`.

### Publication-state contract

The build report gains explicit acquisition provenance. Each acquisition domain records `state` (`not_requested`, `complete`, `partial`, or `failed`), its source, record counts, and machine-readable failure reasons. Renderers derive public copy from provenance plus domain records; record count alone is never used to infer whether acquisition ran.

`scripts/validate_dip_protocol.py` owns dossier-level acquisition facts because it performs or reuses the acquisitions. `scripts/build_dip_pulse_site.py` validates and aggregates those facts into `data/features.json`; renderers consume the validated structure but do not invent acquisition state. All shown top-level objects, all seven domain keys, and every base domain field are required. Summary counters are required only for `summaries`; `items` is required only for `dossiers`. `generated_at` and every non-null domain timestamp are UTC RFC 3339. `generated_at` is publication time. `acquired_at` is when the oldest record contributing to the displayed domain result was successfully fetched or generated; reused records retain their original acquisition time, and derived domains inherit the oldest contributing input time. It is `null` when no usable record exists. `attempted_at` is the current build's acquisition/generation attempt time and is `null` when `attempted` is false. `source_updated_at` is the upstream timestamp when provided and otherwise `null`. Status copy shows “Daten abgerufen: {acquired_at}” when known, “Letzter Versuch: {attempted_at}” for partial or failed attempts, and either “Quellstand: {source_updated_at}” or “Kein Quellstand von der Quelle angegeben”; it never refreshes a cached record's age merely because a build read it.

Count fields are non-negative integers. `records` counts valid aggregate records, `reused` counts valid records read from cache, and `rejected` counts requested records rejected or unresolved. `source` is one of the six values shown in the example. `attempted` is `false` for `not_requested`, derived data, and completely reused cached data; it is `true` when any network acquisition or generation was tried. Producers reject missing required fields, unknown enum values, inconsistent counts, and unknown failure codes. Consumers ignore unknown fields and future domain keys for forward compatibility.

Every catalog record has exactly one dossier `items` entry. Its required fields are `document_number`, `presentation_state`, nullable `report_path`, nullable `page_path`, and `failure_reasons`. A `ready` item requires both relative paths and no failure reason; `unavailable` requires null paths and at least one reason; `not_requested` requires null paths and no failure reason. Catalog rows consume this map directly and never infer dossier state from file existence.

The complete failure-code vocabulary for schema v2 is `source_unavailable`, `provider_timeout`, `invalid_payload`, `invalid_citations`, `duplicate_citation`, `missing_target`, `page_out_of_bounds`, `partial_fetch`, `empty_required_dataset`, `source_changed`, `summary_budget_exhausted`, `unsafe_url`, and `unsafe_output_path`. Public copy groups these into clear domain-level source, validation, completeness, or safety messages rather than exposing implementation detail. Exception messages, provider responses, rejected URLs, and local paths are logged only to build stderr and never enter the manifest or public page.

The domain model has two separate enums:

- `AcquisitionState = not_requested | complete | partial | failed` records pipeline truth.
- `PresentationState = ready | domain_empty | partial | unavailable | omitted` records the public result.

The mapping is deterministic. `complete` with one or more valid records is `ready`; `complete` with zero valid records is `domain_empty` only for votes, profiles, bills, and summaries; `partial` or a mixture of valid and rejected records is `partial`; `failed` is `unavailable`; and `not_requested` is `unavailable`, except summaries are `omitted`. Roster `complete` with zero records is invalid and becomes `failed` with `empty_required_dataset`; roster `not_requested` is valid and `unavailable`. Valid cached records use the same mapping and report `attempted: false` plus `reused > 0`. Any rejected summary is omitted at card level even when other summaries are valid; the aggregate summaries domain is then `partial`.

“Meaningful data” therefore means a record that passed the domain validator and can be linked to its named provenance source. “Ordinary public build” means the default static-site build without `--include-dev-view`; it can reuse cached enrichment and need not access the network.

The catalog is the one required non-empty dataset. `source_unavailable`, `invalid_payload`, or `empty_required_dataset` on the catalog produces exit status `1` and makes the output directory undeployable. Those codes are non-fatal for an individual requested dossier: its XML is excluded, the aggregate dossier state becomes `partial` or `failed`, and valid catalog and dossier pages still render. `--detail-limit -1` deliberately records dossiers as `not_requested` and is a valid catalog-only publication. No empty dossier page is generated. Votes and derived bills may validly be empty; profiles may validly have zero matches; summaries may validly have zero eligible TOPs. No other failure code is fatal unless `--summary-mode required` makes summary completeness mandatory.

Acquisition facts are validated before the affected public pages are rendered. Keep the existing in-place output strategy for `0.3.x`, but define its directory as single-writer and disposable: a failed build may leave partial files, and deployment may consume the directory only after exit status `0` plus manifest and public-output validation. Never serve or deploy an output tree while its build is running. Atomic staging and promotion remain a separately reviewed reliability project. Invalid CLI or configuration input exits `2` before acquisition or writes. Tests inject renderer/write failures and prove that the build exits nonzero and the deployment validator rejects the result. Tests inspect generated HTML and JSON presentation surfaces for developer-only markers and local paths without scanning arbitrary cached source text or binary artifacts.

Conditional relation sections with no matching records are omitted on aggregate landing, pulse, bill, and MP pages; they do not display a warning merely for having no relation. The exact domain-empty copy is reserved for the dedicated dossier vote/bill positions and the bills index, where an empty result answers an explicit visitor question.

Warnings never collapse different domains into one state. When several appear in one section, order is dossiers, votes, bills, roster, profiles, then summaries. Roster copy describes MP-catalog completeness; profile copy describes external biography-link coverage. One never overrides the other.

### Datenstand contract

Do not generate a public `status.html`. `sources.html#datenstand` contains a compact reader-facing block for votes, profiles, roster, and AI summaries. It shows the localized publication time separately from each domain's immutable acquisition time, the last attempt time when a current attempt was partial or failed, a named allowlisted source link, and one of “Verfügbar,” “Keine passenden Daten,” “Teilweise verfügbar,” “Nicht verfügbar,” or “Nicht veröffentlicht.” Page-local dossier and bill notices remain beside affected content because those domains are not reported by this compact block; diagnostic valid/reused/rejected counters stay in `data/features.json`.

Summary counters remain mutually consistent: `generated + reused + omitted + failed = eligible`, `records = generated + reused`, and `rejected = failed`. `fallbacks` is a subset of `reused`; transient provider exceptions stay in stderr. The command-line build summary prints generated, reused, omitted, failed, and fallback counts plus the exact `--summary-mode required` remedy when incomplete summaries must fail the build.

### AI-summary contract

- `--summary-mode reuse|off|auto|required` remains an operator control. `--enrich all` continues not to imply paid summary generation.

| Mode | Read valid cache | Call an LLM | Published result | Failure behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `reuse` (default) | Yes | Never | Publish each valid cached summary; omit missing/invalid ones | Record omissions; build succeeds |
| `off` | No | Never | Omit all AI summaries, including cached ones | Record `not_requested`; build succeeds |
| `auto` | Yes, as fallback | Yes, for every eligible TOP | Prefer a newly generated valid summary; use a valid cached summary when generation fails; otherwise omit | Record per-TOP failures; build succeeds |
| `required` | Yes, as fallback | Yes, for every eligible TOP | Publish a new valid summary or a valid cached fallback for every eligible TOP | Preflight rejects an insufficient minimum call budget before provider calls; any remaining incomplete TOP makes the build exit `1` and the output undeployable |

A TOP is eligible when the source contains at least three distinct speeches with non-empty text that can each produce a citation target. Provider credentials are required only for `auto` and `required`; missing credentials make `auto` record failures and continue, while `required` exits `1` before provider calls. `auto` and `required` use `--summary-max-calls` (default `25`, `0` disables calls) as a hard budget across actual provider HTTP requests, including fallback models. Requests run sequentially with a configurable timeout defaulting to 60 seconds and no automatic transport retry; a fallback model is the only second attempt and consumes another budget unit. `required` preflights that the budget can fund at least one attempt for each eligible TOP lacking a valid cache; later fallback exhaustion still fails the build. `auto` records `summary_budget_exhausted` and omits remaining summaries without exceeding the budget.
- A usable summary has non-empty text and between three and five distinct cited source chunks. Each chunk has a unique non-empty `id`, non-empty excerpt `text`, optional `rede_id`, and optional positive integer `source_page`; it must have at least one of the two target fields. Validation first resolves `rede_id` to an existing speech anchor. If that fails, it resolves `source_page` to `pdf_url#page=N`; when PDF page-count metadata exists, `N` must also be within bounds. Duplicate IDs or duplicate resolved targets, an unresolvable chunk, a missing PDF URL for a page fallback, or fewer than three valid chunks invalidates the whole summary. The build records `invalid_citations` plus the specific safe code `duplicate_citation`, `missing_target`, or `page_out_of_bounds` and emits a warning.
- Summary cache validity is source-bound. For each eligible TOP, compute a canonical SHA-256 over the normalized TOP identity and heading plus the exact ordered prompt chunks (`id`, `rede_id`, `source_page`, speaker, and normalized text). Store that `source_fingerprint` beside `summary_schema_version`, `prompt_version`, provider, and model. Reuse requires every field plus a current fingerprint match and usable citations. Legacy entries without these fields, changed-source entries, and unknown schema/prompt versions are invalid caches: `reuse` omits them with a warning, while `auto`/`required` may regenerate them under the call budget. A generated summary is written to cache only after full structural validation.
- When no usable summary exists, the page contains no empty AI card. `sources.html#datenstand` explains summary coverage separately.
- When a summary exists, its persistent header begins with the exact label “KI-generiert · nicht redaktionell geprüft.”
- Each summary links through the existing stable speech anchor when a `rede_id` exists and otherwise to the source PDF page. This change does not introduce permanent URLs for generated chunk identifiers such as `S1`. The markup includes `data-ai-generated="true"`.
- The disclosure hides the summary prose and evidence list by setting `hidden` on the body element. Its persistent header has one disclosure button. Evidence count and method are ordinary links inside the expanded body, so provenance never competes with the sole show/hide action.
- All summaries start expanded on a first visit. One explicitly global action persists `expanded|collapsed` under `bundestag-pulse-ai-summaries-v1`; `presentation.ai_summary_default` is `expanded`. Malformed/unavailable storage and JavaScript-disabled visits always leave server-expanded content visible.
- Server markup starts expanded: each body lacks `hidden`, each disclosure button has `aria-expanded="true"`, and target IDs remain deterministic and collision-safe. The head bootstrap may read the single-purpose preference inside `try/catch`, but collapsed styling takes effect only after the controller has attached listeners, synchronized `hidden` and `aria-expanded`, and set a controller-ready marker. Controller failure, unavailable or malformed storage, and no JavaScript therefore leave content visible; print always expands it.
- Every disclosure label makes its global scope explicit with `Alle KI-Zusammenfassungen …`; all controls synchronize to the persisted state. Cross-tab synchronization is not required. Native buttons provide Enter and Space activation, and focus stays on the activating button.
- The bootstrap replaces, and does not preserve, the general feature bootstrap. Print CSS overrides `hidden` for summary bodies and hides disclosure controls. No disclosure animation is required, so reduced-motion behavior is unchanged.

### Engineering contract

- Retain the component protocol and lazy loader for enrichment, persistence, page writing, dossier sections, styles, and scripts.
- Replace the mixed visitor/build `Feature` selection with a small operator-only `Enrichment` definition for `votes`, `aw-profiles`, and `mp-roster`. It retains only pipeline metadata needed for acquisition and lazy loading. `EnrichmentSelection` validates and deduplicates those three IDs; it never decides which public pages or sections exist.
- Public components for dossiers, bills, MP pages, votes, and source views load unconditionally. Acquisition provenance decides whether each applicable section shows data, an empty state, a coverage note, or a failure. Summary modes remain separate from enrichment selection.
- Add a small standard-library `scripts/publication_state.py` module as the sole schema-v2 boundary. It defines the enums, required fields, count/timestamp invariants, acquisition-to-presentation derivation, safe failure codes, and serialization. Acquisition producers create facts through it, the builder aggregates and validates through it, and renderers receive validated immutable dictionaries; no producer or renderer independently infers state from record counts or file existence.
- `--include-dev-view` is a separate boolean passed only to development rendering. It never enters enrichment selection or ordinary public component loading.
- Delete `default_visible`, `client_mode`, visitor-facing `nav_key`, feature CSS, browser dependency cascades, and public render-entry-point selection parameters. Keep the `scripts/features/` package name and component protocol in `0.3.x` to avoid unrelated import churn.
- Remove public `data-feature` gating, `feature_bootstrap_script()`, the global feature runtime, settings switches, settings panel, and their general dependency-resolution tests.
- Keep `data/features.json` during `0.3.x`, give it `schema_version: 2`, remove visitor-visibility and storage-key fields, and add fixed-presentation plus per-domain acquisition state. There are no production consumers in this repository, so do not generate an alias. A possible rename to `data/publication-status.json` is a separately reviewed `0.4.0` concern.
- Keep `features.json`, `features.local.json`, `--features-file`, `--enrich`, and `BUNDESTAG_PULSE_ENRICHMENTS` for `0.3.x`, but document them strictly as operator acquisition configuration. Renaming those interfaces is outside this release.
- Replace the developer-view use of general feature flags with one explicit `--include-dev-view` build flag. It requires an output root explicitly marked for development and writes `build.development_output: true`; it refuses the ordinary publication output root. The public-output validator and CI reject any artifact carrying that marker. Default and CI publication builds omit the module and its markup. A generated-output test fails if an ordinary build contains `.dev-only`, `data-feature-dev-view`, `file://`, absolute `/Users/` or `/home/` paths, or known raw-cache payload markers.

Operator acquisition precedence is implemented as one ordered stream of replace/add/remove operations carrying source provenance. The default is no optional enrichment. The order is: repository `features.json`; replacing `enrich` in `features.local.json`; replacing `enrich` in `--features-file`; translated legacy environment input; canonical `BUNDESTAG_PULSE_ENRICHMENTS`; translated legacy CLI replacement/add/remove operations; repeated canonical `--enrich`; and finally implicit `votes` from `--vote-scan-pages > 0`. Within one legacy source, replacement runs first, additions second, and removals last; across sources, the later canonical source wins, so an explicit canonical `--enrich mp-roster` can supersede legacy `--no-roster`. An empty replacement selects none; `all` means all three non-LLM enrichments (`votes`, `aw-profiles`, `mp-roster`); duplicates are silently deduplicated. `--explain-config` resolves this stack without network access or writes and prints both winning and overridden operations with their sources, including implicit vote selection. Unknown IDs, contradictory operations within a canonical source, and invalid JSON exit `2` before network access. A pairwise precedence matrix tests every legacy/canonical positive and negative combination.

Ordinary-build tests scan generated HTML and JSON presentation files for `.dev-only`, `data-feature-dev-view`, `data-dev-view`, raw developer payload markers, `file://`, and absolute local paths. The check reports relative file, rule name, and line number. It does not scan SQLite, binary assets, arbitrary source text, or cached parliamentary payloads; a general whole-tree scanner is outside this release.

All untrusted URLs and generated paths pass shared publication-boundary validators before rendering or writing. Public external links require `https`, an explicit per-source host allowlist, and no embedded credentials; invalid links are omitted with a safe source action or make a required source record invalid. Relative output paths are normalized, must remain beneath the selected output root, and reject absolute paths, `..`, backslashes, encoded traversal, and control characters. Existing `html.escape(..., quote=True)` remains the final markup-escaping layer, but escaping is not treated as URL authorization. Tests use malicious URL, identifier, filename, and traversal fixtures.

The output root is a single-writer build workspace. A new read-only validation command checks schema v2, `development_output: false`, expected entry points, public-output denylist rules, and confined relative paths. Deployment is contractually allowed to upload only a completed exit-0 workspace that passes this validator; because this repository has no deployment workflow, documentation and CI expose the command without inventing a platform-specific pipeline.

### Architecture

The architecture is explicit and deliberately small. `publication_state.py` is a pure standard-library validation/derivation boundary, not a service layer or generalized platform.

```text
operator inputs
 files → env → legacy aliases → canonical CLI → implicit vote
                         │
                         ▼
              ordered config operations
                         │
                         ▼
                 EnrichmentSelection
                votes / profiles / roster
                         │
       ┌─────────────────┴─────────────────┐
       ▼                                   ▼
DIP/XML/AW acquisition              LLM summary modes
validate_dip_protocol.py       off/reuse/auto/required
       │                         │  source fingerprint
       │ acquisition facts       │  budget + timeout
       └──────────────┬──────────┘
                      ▼
          publication_state.py
 enums + invariants + derivation + safe reasons
                      │
                      ▼
          build_dip_pulse_site.py
 aggregate → validate → serialize schema v2
                      │
        ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
        ▼                           ▼
 render_dip_pulse_html.py      data/features.json
 fixed public components       tooling/status contract
        │                           │
        └─────────────┬─────────────┘
                      ▼
          disposable output workspace
                      │
         exit 0 + artifact validation
                      │
                      ▼
                 deployable artifact
```

The component protocol remains the extension seam. Public components no longer depend on selection state; operator enrichments affect facts, not page existence. The builder remains the orchestrator but loses schema derivation, URL/path authorization, and mixed configuration semantics to small focused helpers. Inline ASCII comments should accompany the ordered configuration-operation reducer and publication-state derivation table; ordinary render functions do not need diagrams.

Accepted architecture decisions:

1. Keep in-place generation for `0.3.x`, explicitly treat output as single-writer/disposable, and require exit 0 plus artifact validation before deployment. Atomic promotion is deferred because it is a separate reliability mechanism, not necessary to separate the visitor model.
2. Use immutable `acquired_at`, nullable current-build `attempted_at`, and publication `generated_at`; cache reads never refresh acquisition age.
3. Bind summary reuse to a canonical SHA-256 of the exact normalized prompt source plus schema/prompt/provider/model metadata.
4. Add a hard 25-request default LLM budget, 60-second per-request timeout, sequential execution, and no transport retries. This caps spend and concurrency while retaining explicit override.
5. Validate HTTPS/host allowlists and output-path confinement at the publication boundary; retain HTML escaping as a separate final defense.
6. Resolve configuration as one provenance-carrying operation stream with a fully tested precedence matrix.

### Performance

- Summary generation is the dominant slow/costly path. Sequential calls avoid provider bursts; the call budget bounds cost, and the timeout bounds each attempt. The CLI reports consumed/remaining calls and generated/reused/omitted/failed/fallback counts.
- Rendering should remain linear in catalog, dossier, speech, and relationship counts. The 10× offline fixture guards against accidentally rescanning all dossiers for every page or embedding whole catalogs into every output. No concurrency or cache layer is added until evidence demands it.

### What already exists

- `publication_selection()` already selects all components; reuse that behavior while deleting browser visibility semantics.
- The feature component protocol and lazy loader already isolate enrichment hooks and page contributions; retain them.
- `html.escape(..., quote=True)` already handles markup escaping; add URL authorization rather than replacing escaping.
- DIP acquisition already has timeout/retry behavior; keep it domain-specific instead of imposing the LLM policy on it.
- SQLite already demonstrates temporary replacement, but whole-publication staging is intentionally not generalized in `0.3.x`.
- The standard-library `unittest` fixtures and Python-version CI matrix provide the test foundation; no new framework is needed.

### NOT in scope

- Atomic/versioned whole-publication promotion: valuable when a real deploy pipeline or concurrent writer appears, but it requires its own lifecycle and rollback design.
- Semantic AI evaluation or editorial approval workflow: structural provenance cannot establish factual support, balance, or correctness.
- Deployment-provider configuration: none exists in this repository; the feature supplies a portable validation handoff.
- A generalized security scanner: validation is restricted to generated public HTML/JSON, outbound source links, and generated paths touched by this migration.
- Concurrency for provider calls or page generation: bounded sequential behavior is safer until scale data justifies complexity.
- New services, dependencies, database migrations, analytics, accounts, alerts, package renames, or public status infrastructure.
- Repository license/community-governance selection: important project debt, but a separate legal/product decision.

### Product outcome and initial audience

The initial audience is a politically interested citizen who wants to understand one recent Bundestag event without learning the DIP data model. The job is: identify what was debated, see the connected speakers/documents/votes, and verify an important statement against a primary source.

The release is successful when a representative visitor can complete these three tasks on desktop and mobile without opening settings or understanding “Bausteine”:

1. Reach the latest dossier and identify its main agenda items.
2. Find an applicable vote, linked Drucksache, or speaker profile when that data is present, and correctly understand a missing-data message when it is not.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enrichment configuration | Unknown ID or invalid JSON | Yes | Exit `2` before network access with migration guidance | Existing publication unchanged by the command. |
| Votes/profile/roster fetch | Source unavailable or partial result | Yes | Preserve validated cached rows; record explicit state and safe reason | Verified data renders with one contextual warning, or “not fetched.” |
| Summary storage read/write | Unavailable or malformed `localStorage` | Yes | Use in-memory expanded default | Summary remains usable; preference is not persisted. |
| Summary generation | Missing key/provider error | Yes except `required` | `reuse/off/auto/required` semantics remain; invalid output omitted | No empty AI card; source-backed content is unaffected. |
| Summary citation target resolution | Missing/duplicate/out-of-bounds target | Yes | Reject only that summary and log a safe diagnostic | AI card omitted; primary evidence remains. |
| Developer component inclusion | Flag omitted | Yes | Do not load component; generated-HTML assertion enforces absence | No developer output in ordinary pages. |
| Renderer/browser regression | All formerly hidden blocks render together | Yes | Fixture, responsive, keyboard, print, and no-JS checks | Release blocks on failure. |

No new rescue mechanism is added for pre-existing partial-directory writes; that independent reliability gap is recorded out of scope rather than obscured.

### Section 3 — Security and threat model

The material in-scope risk is accidental publication of the developer view or local build paths. Module exclusion plus generated-HTML assertions is the appropriate boundary. API keys remain build-time secrets and are never added to output. AI text is untrusted generated content: renderers continue escaping it, labels remain persistent, and citation targets are validated. Structural citations must not be presented as semantic verification.

### Section 4 — Data flow and interaction edge cases

```text
operator config ──> enrichment selection ──> fetch/reuse ──> report + acquisition facts
                                                               │
public components (always loaded) <── validated report <────────┘
          │
          ├── source-backed content + contextual state copy
          └── usable AI summary ──> labelled expanded/collapsed disclosure
```

Required edge cases are complete data, successful empty lookup, not requested, partial, failed, invalid cached summary, unavailable storage, JavaScript disabled, print, and multiple summary controls. “Not requested” and “complete with zero records” must produce different copy.

### Section 5 — Code quality review

Delete the visitor-only fields `default_visible`, `client_mode`, and `nav_key`, plus the browser manifest/CSS/runtime functions. Prefer a small `Enrichment` record and plain sets over a generalized capability graph. Keep dependency resolution only if an actual enrichment requires it. Renderer entry points stop accepting visibility selections; public module loading is centralized once.

### Section 6 — Test review

```text
operator input -> acquisition state -> report/manifest -> renderer -> browser behavior
      unit              unit             contract          fixture      browser/print
```

P1 tests cover configuration precedence, the four acquisition states plus complete-empty, fixed navigation, absence of general feature state, summary validation/modes, no-JS expanded content, storage failures, keyboard focus, print expansion, and developer-output absence. Three representative fixtures—complete, no optional acquisition, and partial/failed—are sufficient; do not generate the full cross-product.

### Section 7 — Performance review

Removing the feature bootstrap and hidden developer payload improves first-load work and document size. The disclosure controller must be one delegated/page-level controller rather than one listener per evidence node. No new network request occurs in the browser. Capture generated dossier HTML size before/after to ensure the public default does not retain the roughly megabyte-scale hidden developer payload.

### Section 8 — Observability and debuggability review

Build stderr remains the operator diagnostic channel. Safe acquisition states and source-fetch timestamps enter the report/manifest; exception messages and local paths do not. `--list-features` may keep its compatibility alias but the canonical command becomes `--list-enrichments`, whose output names network/cost behavior rather than visitor visibility.

### Section 9 — Deployment and rollout review

Roll out as reversible slices on one branch: fixed presentation first, acquisition-state wording second, AI disclosure third, cleanup last. Before release, inventory repository and deployment references to retired fields/options, rebuild representative offline output, and compare screenshots/print. `settings.html` prevents bookmark breakage. Legacy operator aliases remain through `0.3.x`, so rollback does not require reconstructing removed deployment arguments.

### Section 10 — Long-term trajectory review

Reversibility is **4/5**: static output and compatibility aliases make UI rollback easy; report-shape changes require fixture discipline. The plan moves toward the 12-month ideal by making the connected dossier the product and by separating pipeline truth from visitor choice. It deliberately does not claim to solve freshness operations, distribution, or semantic AI evaluation.

### Section 11 — Design and UX review

The information hierarchy serves the visitor when source-backed content is primary, AI is visibly secondary, and data-state copy appears only near the affected section. Remove the gear entirely. Keep public destinations stable. Use a real disclosure button with `aria-expanded` and `hidden`; retain persistent AI provenance and an evidence action while collapsed. On mobile, controls wrap without hiding labels; touch targets are at least 44×44 CSS pixels. With JavaScript disabled and in print, summaries remain expanded and evidence links remain usable.

## Cross-phase themes

- **Remove false choices, preserve real controls** — CEO, Design, DX, and Eng all distinguish the fixed public experience from legitimate operator acquisition/cost controls. The public Baustein model disappears; `--enrich`, summary modes, and explicit development output remain.
- **Trust requires provenance plus honest failure states** — CEO, Design, and Eng independently require source-first presentation, distinct empty/not-requested/partial/failed states, immutable acquisition age, and no implication that structural citation validation proves truth.
- **Fail open for readers, fail closed for publication** — Design and Eng converge on server-expanded/no-JS-readable content while rejecting unsafe, developer-marked, incomplete, or failed output at the deployment boundary.
- **A clean first success must also be a real acceptance fixture** — Design and DX use Plenarprotokoll 21/84 TOP 32a/32b; Eng turns it into the deterministic zero-network demo and integrated release test.
